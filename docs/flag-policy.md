# Wording and motive flags: what deserves an admin's attention

Derived from the project owner's labels on 26 real Wording/Motive flags (eval/labels/wording_motive_user_labels.json, 2026-11-14). Admin-tier precision before this policy: 12/22 (55%, excluding unsure and duplicate items). These are editorial rules, not phrase lists; they must hold on fresh cases.

## Flag (the edit changes what a reader would believe)
- Removes a qualifier, caveat, attribution or the other side of a dispute that the text or source carried.
- Adds a cause, purpose or motive ("to …", "due to …", "in order to …") that is not supported anywhere in the cited source. Check the WHOLE cited document (Exa highlights for the claim), not only the passage first shown: if another part of the source states it, do not flag.
- Adds editorial voice: commentary on the subject in Wikipedia's own voice ("ironically", "notably", "unfortunately", "it should be noted").
- Replaces a neutral noun or label with an evaluative one ("club" -> "powerhouse", "a city" -> "the best city in the world").
- Adds non-encyclopedic content: first-person text, appeals or messages to readers, test edits, personal opinions. Content touching politically or socially sensitive topics (wars, ethnic groups, living people) is the highest priority.

## Do not flag as Wording/Motive
- Size, rank or scale claims ("one of the largest … in Europe"): these are factual claims; at most a FACT check against a source, never a wording flag.
- Mild intensifiers and descriptors that do not change the meaning ("fiercely", "sprawling", "prime examples", "exhaustive").
- Informal register or style problems ("trash itself"): copy-editing, not bias.
- The same problem already flagged elsewhere in the edit: one flag per problem.
