Design an interface for NeoWiki, a tool for exploring how Wikipedia language editions differ over time.

Audience: nontechnical readers, Wikipedia editors, and hackathon judges using a normal laptop. Within seconds, someone should understand the scale of disagreement, find an interesting question, and open its supporting evidence.

Core journey: choose an article and languages -> explore differences over time -> select a specific semantic question -> inspect exact article evidence.

Choose the visualization form yourself. Do not assume lines, bubbles, maps or heatmaps. Optimize for comprehension and meaningful navigation rather than novelty alone.

Data meaning:
- One item is one underlying semantic question with an observed cross-language difference. Repeated passages, pairs and checkpoints must not inflate its count.
- Questions are generated from discovered disagreement groups, not a preset list of questions the system attempts to answer. Examples: Who initiated the invasion? How many Finnish soldiers died? What territorial terms were agreed?
- Categories are additional context, different framing, and possible contradiction. These are model judgments for human review, not truth or bias scores.
- A month/year count is distinct questions observed during that period. The same question can recur across months, so monthly counts need not sum to the year total.
- Historical assessments are sampled. Distinguish unassessed periods, absent findings, assessed zero results, and dates outside source coverage. Do not imply continuity, agreement or resolution across gaps.

Design requirements:
- One dominant visualization at a time, comfortably usable at 1280x720.
- Move naturally between broad historical periods and individual questions.
- Support hundreds of questions without squeezing every label on screen or silently capping findings.
- Keep search available on demand: immediate keyword results, then semantic refinement.
- Reveal concise details through hover, keyboard focus and touch.
- Make zoom/drilldown, going back, and preserving context obvious.
- Evidence is a focused next step, with original excerpts, translations, highlights, actual language pairs, dates and revision links. Keep the current edition first when present; FI-SV evidence must remain valid without English.

Visual direction: calm, editorial, precise and contemporary, with a subtle connection to Wikipedia's reading clarity. Warm ivory, charcoal and restrained amber/orange/red data colors are welcome. Avoid dashboards with simultaneous charts, crowded toolbars, excessive panels, unsupported severity scores, and decoration that obscures meaning.

Deliver one recommended direction, explain each visual encoding, and show the overview, detailed exploration and evidence transition. Include laptop/mobile layouts and loading, empty and unassessed states. Label illustrative data honestly. Prioritize a convincing first version over elaborate interaction or visual spectacle.
