# Library visual direction

Approved direction: an Interstellar-like lattice of tangible shelves and books, with Library of Babel vastness and guided continuous zoom. The owner selected this world and its interaction over several question rounds; the later design seed cannot replace those pinned decisions. No further direction selection is pending.

World: warm ivory text and oxidised brass light against deep graphite architecture. Books carry restrained terracotta, ochre and blue-green cloth colours; actual article identity is always labelled. Great depth comes from repeated structural shelves, distant light, perspective and occlusion. Architecture carries the first viewport; controls occupy quiet edges.

The overview looks down and through the impossible library. Selecting a topic resolves into an eye-height, warmly lit corridor. Each edition appears on one side in an alternating ordered sequence. The dated books are the focal objects; distant shelving supplies atmosphere without inventing content. Guided motion is smooth, interruptible and stops completely for reading. Reduced motion uses direct navigation.

Typography: self-hosted literary serif for article/title voices, paired with a readable sans for controls; tabular dates and edition indices. Reader: one large, paper-coloured book surface with a subtle central binding and an HTML article at a comfortable reading measure. Native revision colours retain their established last-change meaning.

Controls have explicit labels, visible focus and clear loading/empty/error states. Desktop scene-first view; narrow and reduced-motion views use an accessible catalog/revision list. The same full reader and inline history work in either route. Closing returns to the prior camera position and focus.

Quality proof: inspect the actual desktop scene, real article reader and narrow fallback together; repair the concrete findings in one batch and confirm once. Measure scene walking performance. A screenshot is not API or provenance evidence.
